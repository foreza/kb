# Summary 

- Negotiation can be applied to literally everything. Negotiation is life. 
- Being able to negotiate well takes lots of preparation as well as flexibility.
- Negotiation is often discovery as well.
- Emotions can be used to build empathy with the person - don't surpress or disregard them. They are just as important as the information itself. 
- Use labeling as a tool to validate emotions. Can also stragegically mislabel on purpose to test for engagement. "It seems that..."
- Black swans are "deal killers", find them and leverage them. 
- People often appear irrational because you are missing information.
- Have multiple people involved as they provide backup / pick up different pieces of information.
- Humans do not listen well, we selectively process what we want to process. We are terrible listeners.
- Getting a strong "no" is progress as it shows engagement. It's better than a fake "yes". 
- Also, be comfortable with silence. Let the person talk. Observe them. The whole package (the information, what they say, what they do, how they act) is all a bunch of information you can utilize. 
- Make your counterpart feel safe first so you can discover intent. If both of you come in with talking just about your agenda, you will get nowhere. We can "repeat" or "mirror" somebody as well.
- Your voice and your demeanor matter more that the content in a LOT of cases - use them well.
- Building a rapport with someone, spending face time with them allows you to better figure out how to make deals. 
- It is even more important to negotiate for yourself and not compromise.
- Approach things from a position of honest curiosity, look to succeed together
- We are all biased - we need to understand our biases.
- 3 different (loose categorizations?) types of negotiators. Accommodators, analysts, and assertive types.
